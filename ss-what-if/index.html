How this is figured

  • Only the Social Security part of FICA (called OASDI) is counted. The Medicare part is left out. Rates and the yearly earnings cap are the real ones for each year, including the 2011–2012 payroll tax holiday. Self-employed tax uses 92.35% of net earnings after 1990; the 1984–1989 self-employment credits are approximated.
  • Each year's tax goes in mid-year and earns half that year's return, then full returns after that. The fund is rebalanced every year and the fee is taken out yearly.
  • Market returns are S&P 500 total returns with dividends reinvested, 1980–2025. Bonds are 10-year U.S. Treasury total returns. 2026 uses the S&P figure above and about 4% for bonds. Later years use the return you set.
  • The Social Security estimate uses the SSA formula: your top 35 years of wage-indexed earnings, the bend points for the year you turn 62, and cost-of-living raises since then (2.5%, 2.8%, and 3.6% for 2027). Your SSA statement is more accurate, so enter it in Step 4 if you have it.
  • This is a "what if," not advice. Social Security also pays disability, survivor, and spouse benefits and rises with inflation for life. A private account can run out or be left to your heirs. Taxes on withdrawals are not included.
