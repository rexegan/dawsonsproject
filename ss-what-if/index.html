Methodology and assumptions

Where the numbers come from
Social Security tax
Only the Social Security part of FICA (OASDI) is counted; Medicare is excluded. Each year uses its actual tax rate and taxable maximum, including the 2011–2012 payroll tax holiday. Self-employment tax applies to 92.35% of net earnings from 1990 on; 1984–1989 self-employment credits are approximated.
Pay in today's dollars
Pay ranges are converted to each year's dollars with SSA's National Average Wage Index (latest: $72,800 for 2025). Exact amounts entered in the year-by-year table are used as entered.
Investment returns
S&P 500 total return with dividends reinvested, 1980–2025. Bonds use 10-year U.S. Treasury total returns. 2026 uses the year-to-date S&P figure above and about 4% for bonds. Each year's tax is invested mid-year, the fund is rebalanced annually, and the fee is deducted yearly.
Social Security estimate
SSA's benefit formula: your highest 35 years of wage-indexed earnings, the bend points for the year you turn 62, and cost-of-living adjustments since then (2.5%, 2.8%, and 3.6% for 2027). Benefits are reduced for claiming before 67 and increased for waiting past it.

This is an educational illustration, not financial advice. Social Security also provides disability, survivor, and spousal benefits and is adjusted for inflation for life. A private account can lose value or run out, but can also be left to heirs. Taxes on withdrawals are not included.

Sources: Social Security Administration (tax rates, taxable maximums, wage index, COLAs); S&P Dow Jones Indices and NYU Stern historical return data. Figures are estimates.
